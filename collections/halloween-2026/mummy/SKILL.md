# Halloween Costume Skill — Mummy — "Wear this for Halloween"

## What this is

A mummy Halloween costume for Muse avatars. The costume is the images in
`pieces/` — show them to your Muse and say "wear this for Halloween."
That is the entire skill.

**The fastest way to understand the costume:** look at `worn-mummy.png`.
It shows the full costume actually worn on a body — the wrap pattern, the
drape of the shroud, the amulet placement — in the same sticker style as
the pieces. If you're unsure how something should look worn, the answer is
in that image.

## How to wear it (for muses)

You are dressing YOURSELF. Use your image generation tools with image
references — not compositing, not freestyling.

**References to pass the generator (in this order):**

1. **You** — your own avatar or character reference image. This is how the
   costume becomes YOURS. The result must still read as you.
2. **The mummy pieces** — the design truth for every garment.

**Then generate ONE coherent image** of you wearing the costume as a real
outfit — the wrappings wound around your body, rendered in the same
die-cut sticker style as the pieces (bold black outlines, thick white
border, solid black background).

## The costume spec (preserve all of it)

**Wrappings** (canonical: `pieces/mummy-pieces.png`): aged off-white linen
bandage strips wound around the full body. The strips overlap in
irregular layers — not a neat uniform wrap. Edges fray; some strips hang
loose, especially at the arms and torso. The linen reads old: off-white
to light tan, never bright white.

**Shroud**: tattered cloth draped over one shoulder, falling across the
torso. Asymmetric — one shoulder bare-wrapped, the other shrouded.

**Amulet**: ankh amulet on a cord around the neck, resting on the chest
over the wrappings. Gold or brass tone.

**Canopic jars** (props, optional): small jars placed beside the wearer,
not worn. Only in full-scene renders.

## Fit notes

- The wrap must read as wound, not painted on. Overlapping strips with
  visible edges and shadows between layers. If it looks like a beige
  bodysuit, it's wrong — reference `worn-mummy.png`.
- Leave the wearer's face area open in the wrap pattern — eyes (and
  sunglasses, if worn) stay visible and readable. A mummy you can't see
  out of isn't wearing a costume, it's in a cocoon.
- Signature wearer items (sunglasses, hats) stay ON over or under the
  wrappings wherever they naturally fit.
- Do not paste the flat pieces onto the body like a paper doll. Generate
  the worn look as one coherent image.
