# Halloween Costume Skill — Sherlock Holmes — "Wear this for Halloween"

## What this is

A Sherlock Holmes Halloween costume for Muse avatars. The costume is the
images in `pieces/` — show them to your Muse and say "wear this for
Halloween." That is the entire skill.

**The fastest way to understand the costume:** look at `worn-sherlock.png`.
It shows the full costume actually worn — the deerstalker fit, the cape
drape — in the same sticker style as the pieces.

## How to wear it (for muses)

You are dressing YOURSELF. Use your image generation tools with image
references — not compositing, not freestyling.

**References to pass the generator (in this order):**

1. **You** — your own avatar or character reference image. This is how the
   costume becomes YOURS. The result must still read as you.
2. **The sherlock pieces** — the design truth for every garment.

**Then generate ONE coherent image** of you wearing the costume as a real
outfit — draped and worn on your body, in the same die-cut sticker style
(bold black outlines, thick white border, solid black background).

## The costume spec (preserve all of it)

**Deerstalker**: the two-flapped hunting cap, worn square on the head. Both
flaps visible — front and back. This is the single most recognizable piece;
get it right.

**Inverness cape**: the cape-coat draped over the shoulders, falling past
the waist. The cape is the silhouette — without it, it's a hat and a pipe.

**Magnifying glass** (prop): held up near the face, the classic inspection
pose. A prop, placed naturally.

**Calabash pipe** (prop): the curved pipe. Held or clenched.

**Vest**: tweed vest under the cape, visible at the front opening.

## Fit notes

- The deerstalker must read as a deerstalker, not a baseball cap. Two
  flaps, front and back — if one flap is missing it collapses into generic
  hat.
- The cape drapes; it doesn't hang like a curtain. Reference the worn ref
  for the shoulder fall.
- Signature wearer items stay ON. The costume adapts to the wearer.
- Do not paste the flat pieces onto the body like a paper doll. Generate
  the worn look as one coherent image.
