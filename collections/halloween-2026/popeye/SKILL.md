# Halloween Costume Skill — Popeye — "Wear this for Halloween"

## What this is

A Popeye the Sailor Halloween costume for Muse avatars (1929 public-domain
design). The costume is the images in `pieces/` — show them to your Muse
and say "wear this for Halloween." That is the entire skill.

**The fastest way to understand the costume:** look at `worn-popeye.png`.
It shows the full costume actually worn on a body — the cap tilt, the
squint, the pipe — in the same sticker style as the pieces.

## How to wear it (for muses)

You are dressing YOURSELF. Use your image generation tools with image
references — not compositing, not freestyling.

**References to pass the generator (in this order):**

1. **You** — your own avatar or character reference image. This is how the
   costume becomes YOURS. The result must still read as you.
2. **The popeye pieces** — the design truth for every garment.

**Then generate ONE coherent image** of you wearing the costume as a real
outfit — draped and worn on your body, in the same die-cut sticker style
(bold black outlines, thick white border, solid black background).

## The costume spec (preserve all of it)

**Cap**: black sailor cap, worn with a slight tilt. Not straight — the tilt
is character.

**Shirt**: sailor collar shirt with red neckerchief. The collar lies flat;
the neckerchief knots at the front.

**The squint**: one eye squinted shut, the classic Popeye squint. This is
non-negotiable — without the squint it's a sailor costume, not Popeye. If
the wearer has sunglasses, the squint reads through the wearer's
expression around them.

**Forearms**: exaggerated forearms, the cartoon kind. Scale them up —
timid forearms ruin the read.

**Pipe**: corncob pipe, clenched in the teeth or held. A prop, placed
naturally.

**Spinach can** (prop, optional): held or tucked at the belt.

## Fit notes

- The squint is the hardest part and the most important. Get it wrong and
  the whole pack fails its two-second recognition test.
- Signature wearer items stay ON. The costume adapts to the wearer.
- Do not paste the flat pieces onto the body like a paper doll. Generate
  the worn look as one coherent image.
